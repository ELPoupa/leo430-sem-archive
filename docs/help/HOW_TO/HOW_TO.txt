$#>KHow to Adjust Focus

Focus is adjusted in order to obtain a sharp image revealing small detail if present in the specimen. Optimum focus may be obstructed through presence of noise so that noise reductionHow_to_Noise_Reduce_and_Freeze_the_Image must be applied. AstigmatismHow_to_Correct_for_Astigmatism may also distort the image and must be corrected.

{bmc bm0.bmp}	ClickCLICK@SEM.HLP on {bmc bm4.bmp} to switch mouse operation to magnification and Working Distance1YCGPIR@SEM.HLP (C3-lens) adjustment.
{bmc bm0.bmp}	With the left mouse button adjust a suitable magnification. A suitable magnification is set when larger details in the image can be seen but smaller details are unsharp. Begin from low magnification upwards.
{bmc bm0.bmp}	With the middle mouse button adjust Working Distance to bring the focus plane to the specimen surface which results in a sharper image.
{bmc bm0.bmp}	Repeat the above two steps at higher magnification if necessary. For an optimum setting adjust focus at a magnification of 5 to 10 times higher than the final magnification in the micrograph will be.

$#>How to Correct for Astigmatism

High resolution images can only be obtained after correct adjustment of astigmatism. Astigmatism (non-roundness of the beam) is caused by either charging of the specimen and applying an asymmetric field that affects the focus of the beam usually in one direction or by a misaligned aperture. 

NOTE It is a good idea to check filament current13HR49B@operation.hlp, align the beam1LKD_PL@operation.hlp and align the final aperture334XEAC@operation.hlp correctly before adjusting stigmation.

{bmc bm0.bmp}	Click on {bmc bm20.bmp} to switch mouse adjustment to stigmation control.
{bmc bm0.bmp}	Focus to optimum focus with the middle mouse button so that the feature does not "smear" in any direction.

$#>KHow to Investigate a Vacuum Leak

A few simple rules:

1.	Before looking for a vacuum leak give the vacuum system time to pump. If you have just changed a specimen in a very humid atmosphere the pump down may take longer. If the specimen is wet it may take several minutes If the stage has been left open for long periods it will take longer to pump.

2.	Vacuum leaks rarely happen by themselves; they are often caused. If you have just done anything to the chamber then that is the most likely cause of the leak. If the specimen has just been changed, check the stage door 'O' ring.

3.	The most frequent cause of leaks is dust or fibers on 'O' rings. 'O' rings may be cleaned with a fluff free tissue. 'O' ring grease should never be used. Grease traps fibers and may cause leaks.

4.	Do not use any metal tools to remove 'O' rings from their grooves. A small scratch in the bottom of a groove causes a big leak.

Checking vacuum levels:
The control of the vacuum system is automatic and software controlled. Actual state of system and high vacuum are displayed on a tab in the SEM Control windowC9V9DR@TabMgr.HLP:
