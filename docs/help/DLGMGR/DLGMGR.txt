$#>K4QBSD Parameters

The data and comments below shall provide for optimal adjustment of all parameters.

Working Distance  The detector has a maximum sensitivity for the back scattered electrons at a working distance of 6 mm if the 10 mm diode pack is fitted or a range of 12 to 20mm if the 25mm diode pack is fitted. At shorter working distance many electrons travel through the central bore  in the detector and at long working distances more and more electrons pass by the sensitive area of the detector diode.  	 	
Tilt Angle  When the surface is oriented normal to the primary electron beam the signal level is the highest and it decreases as the tilt angle increases.  Therefore no strong signal can be expected from a highly tilted specimen surface because the electrons are scattered in forward direction and do not reach the detector anymore. 	{bmc bm4.wmf} 	
High Voltage  The higher the energy of the backscattered electrons the better the collection efficiency.  There is a low KV limit of about 3 to 5 kV below which the detector will not detect backscattered electrons 	 	
In order to obtain the best signal the highest voltage should be selected which the specimen will withstand consistent with good imaging.  Therefore the optimal setting must be considered for each application.

$#>Absorbed Specimen Current Measurement

Principle of Absorbed Specimen Current Measurement:

When the primary electron beam (PE) hits the specimen, a number of different signals are generated from the specimen:  SE: Secondary electrons of low energy BSE: Back scattered electrons of higher energy TE: Transmitted electrons from thin specimens  Some electrons are absorbed in the specimen and must be conducted to ground potential. If the specimen is insulated from ground these electrons can be measured as specimen current (SC) by an amplifier connected between the specimen and the chamber ground.  An equilibrium of charge is built on the specimen giving the following relation of electron currents:    IPE = ISE + IBSE + ITE + ISC  and thus    ISC = IPE - ISE - IBSE - ITE 	  {bmc bm6.wmf}

The primary beam current can be measured with a Faraday cage.

A hole is drilled into the base material of the receptor (1) which has the inner walls painted with carbon to obtain high absorption of electrons. The hole is covered with a diaphragm (2) which is fastened with a spring ring (3).  The primary electron beam (4) is centered into the bore of the diaphragm (2) allowing the electrons to enter the hole (1). There the electrons are being absorbed. Electrons which are reflected (5) cannot leave the Faraday cage and get absorbed on its walls as well.   Thus all electrons are conducted through the Specimen Current Measuring System (6) and are measured there. 	 {bmc bm7.wmf}

$#>Operation Principle of Backscattered Detector
   
While secondary electrons are most often used to display topography contrast the backscattered electrons are used to display the materials contrast because the backscatter coefficient is dependent on the mean atomic number of the material under investigation.   Depth Information Those electrons which form the back scattered image stem from deeper regions of the specimen. The sketches on the right show the principle: The primary electron beam (PE) penetrates under the surface of the specimen because of its high energy. There the electrons interact with the material and are scattered back to the surface (BSE). The mid grey area in the sketch on the left shows approximately the region in which the primary beam is scattered. The real size of the scattering area varies with material and high voltage and serves here as a model only.   On the left a cut an polished specimen is sketched where a dense phase (mid gray) is embedded in a light matrix (white). On the left side the edge of the phase runs perpendicular to the specimen surface, on the right side the edge is inclined. Although the phase is under the specimen surface where the primary electron beam (PE) hits the surface back scattered electrons (BSE) travel to the surface and add to the image. This defocuses the edge more the further the beam travels to the right. On the real image below this effect can be seen at the right edge of the particle very clearly.  Unsharp edges and fuzzy particles are due to the depth effect. Also the problems generated for stereometric evaluation can be seen since the dimensions of the phase can not be identified correctly.   Image: Mineral particle embedded in resin, cut and polished and coated with carbon.	 {bmc bm27.wmf}  {bmc bm28.wmf}  {bmc bm29.bmp}	

$#Registration Algorithm
The algorithm used is two point registration which requires the locations of 2 marks at the extremities of the specimen co-ordinate system to be defined in terms of the stage absolute co-ordinate system.
This algorithm assumes that the user co-ordinate system has the same units in both X and Y directions.

If the user co-ordinate system has different units in X and Y (e.g. rows and columns of structures) then 3 point alignment3_point_alignment may be selected.

For a given registration the Rotation and Tilt axes are constant and the relationship between the XY  co-ordinate systems computed from the positions of the two points in each system

Tilt the sample to produce a stereo effect.

$#Static Stereo Depth Measurement

The depth of a point from the height of a reference point can be calculated by measuring the distance from the point of interest to a reference point on the tilt axis, tilting the sample and measuring the new distance from the point of interest to the reference point. The reference point must not move hence must be on the tilt axis.

Using the Point to Point Cursors place the P1R cursor and the P2R cursor on the reference point on the tilt axis.
Place the P1 cursor on the point of interest.
Tilt the sample by a known number of degrees. The reference point should not move.
Place the P2 cursor on the point of interest (It should have moved ).  
The angle the sample has been tilted can be entered into the St. Stereo Angle parameter.
Press the Calc Static Stereo Depth button.
The  Respective depths as shown on the diagramStatic_Stereo_theory are calculated.

$#specimen current Imaging
The Specimen Current Monitor may be used as an imaging device.
Image Mode
1.	Select the Specimen CurrentSpecimen_Current_Monitor  panel (Tools - Go To Panel and select Specimen Current Monitor)
2.	Obtain a secondary electron image and bring it to focus.
3.	Select SCM from the Detection -  Detectors menu.
4.	Select SCM Image from the Specimen Current panel, set Brightness to 50% and adjust Contrast until an image is seen. Then adjust Brightness and Contrast to obtain the required image.

At low probe currents i.e. on SCM ranges lower than 1nA full scale, the image can become degraded by mains field effects and other forms of interference. Also, on these ranges, the signal bandwidth is very low. The use of long scan times will overcome the bandwidth restrictions and will also help to reduce interference effects, but image quality may not be perfect under these conditions.

$#>KPoints list Text File Format
The stage points list may be saved /loaded as a text file, e.g. for importing into an Excel spreadsheet.

The data is in comma separated format as shown in the following example

Leo Points List
Absolute
Label,X,Y,Z,T,R,M,Mag,WD
2
here,0.01000000,0.01000000,0.00000000,-1.00000000,0.00000000,0.00000000,100.000000,0.00500000
there,0.01500000,0.01500000,0.00500000,0.00000000,0.00000000,0.00000000,100.000000,0.00500000


Line 1 is fixed header Leo Points List to define type of data
Line 2 is either Absolute or Relative (relative if stage registration is used)
Line 3 is column headings (content ignored on loading but line must be present)
Line 4 is count of point data entries which follow

X,Y,Z,M are positions in Metres.
T,R as positions in degrees.
Working distance is in Metres.


If preparing a file to be loaded please note that lines 1 through 4 are mandatory.
